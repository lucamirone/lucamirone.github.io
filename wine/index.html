Importing Italian Wine into the U.S.

A practical roadmap for qualifying the winery, finding the right importer, clearing federal and state gates, approving the label, modeling landed cost, and preparing the first commercial shipment.

🍷
Core principle: the Italian winery normally does not ship commercial wine straight to U.S. retailers. A properly licensed U.S. importer anchors the federal process, and state-by-state distribution and product requirements determine where the wine can actually be sold.

1 · The right mental model

There is no single “wine import approval”

A launch succeeds only when the commercial route, the federal alcohol permits, the FDA food-import layer, the customs entry, the label, and each target state are aligned. The importer is not merely a freight forwarder—it is a regulated commercial counterparty with real legal and financial responsibility.

Common misconception
“The Italian winery can obtain one U.S. approval and sell nationwide.”

Federal approval does not create nationwide distribution rights. State licensing, brand registration, appointments, control-state procedures, taxes, and franchise rules may still control market access.

Correct framework
“Build one compliant federal import platform, then open states deliberately.”

Choose a licensed importer, approve each SKU, clear customs correctly, and launch only in states where importer/distributor/brand requirements are satisfied.

⏳

Time-sensitive tariff alert — verify before every entry

As of July 14, 2026, a temporary 10% ad valorem U.S. import surcharge is scheduled to remain in effect through 12:01 a.m. EDT on July 24, 2026 unless changed or extended. The calculator below includes 10% as an editable planning input. A customs broker must confirm the current Chapter 99 treatment, product exclusions, valuation, and entry date.

Important difference from many foods: qualifying alcoholic beverages are generally exempt from FDA’s Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP). That does not remove FDA food-facility registration, U.S.-agent, Prior Notice, admissibility, or recordkeeping obligations.

3 · Responsibility map

Who owns each part of the transaction?

🇮🇹

Italian winery / producer

  • Truthful formulation, origin, vintage, varietal, ABV, CO₂, sulfite, and claim data
  • FDA facility registration and U.S. agent when required
  • EORI, EU export/excise records, lot traceability
  • Production, label printing, documentation, samples, and market support
🏛️

U.S. TTB importer

  • Federal Basic Importer’s Permit and dealer registration
  • COLA/formula submissions and compliant importer statement
  • Alcohol records, trade-practice compliance, importer agreements
  • Often—but not automatically—the customs importer of record
📦

Customs importer / broker

  • HTS and Chapter 99 classification, customs value, entry, bond
  • Federal excise tax, duties, MPF/HMF, FDA data and Prior Notice coordination
  • CBP/FDA communications, examinations, releases, and corrections
  • A broker files; legal responsibility remains with the applicable importer
🗺️

State wholesaler / distributor

  • State authority to distribute and sell to licensed accounts
  • Brand appointments, territory, price posting, reporting, excise tax as applicable
  • Sales force, inventory placement, depletion data, collections, and local execution
  • Structure differs by state and control-state system
Do not assume the roles are interchangeable. The TTB permit holder, customs importer of record, brand registrant, state licensee, warehouse owner, distributor, and party that owns inventory may be one company—or several. Put the responsibility and economics in writing before filing labels or shipping wine.
4 · Regulatory checklist

Federal, Italian/EU, and state requirements

The following is the standard planning framework for commercially imported, bottled grape wine at 7% alcohol by volume or more. Product facts and the target state may change the path.

Italian / European Union

Export readiness

  1. Legal exporter identity and EORI number.
  2. EU export declaration through the applicable customs process; retain MRN and proof of exit.
  3. Excise movement through EMCS when required, including e-AD/ARC for duty-suspension movements or the applicable duty-paid process.
  4. Commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, lot and case data.
  5. Denomination/origin documentation for DOC, DOCG, IGT, varietal, vintage, and estate claims.
  6. Organic export documentation, including the U.S. NOP Import Certificate when applicable.
U.S. federal

TTB + FDA + CBP

  1. U.S. importer holds a TTB Basic Importer’s Permit and registers as an alcohol dealer.
  2. Wine classification, formula if required, and Certificate of Label Approval (COLA).
  3. Foreign food facility registration, accepted U.S. agent, and biennial renewal where required.
  4. FDA Prior Notice for each shipment; FDA admissibility review remains possible.
  5. Customs importer of record, power of attorney, customs bond, accurate HTS/Chapter 99, value, origin, and entry data.
  6. Pay base duty, any additional tariff, federal excise tax, MPF, HMF when applicable, and other charges.
State and local

Actual market access

  1. Confirm importer/wholesaler/distributor licenses for each target state.
  2. Determine brand or product registration, label filing, and fee requirements.
  3. Appoint distributor and review territory, exclusivity, franchise, termination, and successor rules.
  4. Address price posting, control-state listing, taxes, reporting, warehouse and delivery restrictions.
  5. Confirm tasting, sampling, event, advertising, retailer support, and tied-house rules.
  6. Review local permits and tax obligations where applicable.
7% ABV or more

Ordinary interstate wine

Generally follows TTB wine labeling and COLA rules, plus the government health warning, FDA facility/Prior Notice layer, customs entry, and state requirements.

↔
Under 7% ABV

Different label regime

Generally falls under FDA food-label rules rather than FAA Act wine COLA rules, while TTB tax/production rules, the health warning at 0.5% ABV or more, customs, and state alcohol laws can still apply.

EU wine certification simplification: ordinary qualifying wine from Italy benefits from the U.S.–EU wine agreement, which generally removes the separate “proper cellar treatment” certification otherwise required for certain imported natural wines. This does not excuse formula, label, customs, FDA, origin, or state requirements.

6 · Economics

Cost breakdown: government charges, service costs, and channel margins

The base customs duty on many bottled wines is small compared with freight, the current temporary surcharge, excise tax cash flow, state compliance, and the margins required by importer, distributor, and retailer. Model the shelf price before pursuing importers.

Federal fee: $0

TTB permit, COLA, formula

TTB does not charge an application fee for the federal importer permit, COLA, or formula submission. Consultants and attorneys may charge for preparation.

Federal fee: $0

FDA facility registration

FDA does not charge a registration fee. A private U.S. agent, registrar, or consultant may charge a recurring commercial fee.

Varies widely

States and private services

State licenses/registrations, broker, bond, labels, freight, warehousing, insurance, samples, compliance support, and distributor terms are market costs—not fixed federal fees.

Editable landed-cost calculator

Use this for planning—not customs filing. Entered value, HTS, tariff layers, tax class, CBMA assignment, state charges, and Incoterm must be confirmed for the actual shipment.

Every editable control includes a detailed bilingual explanation. Open the information link below a field, or expand all descriptions at once.

Preset buttons change only the starting assumptions for federal tax class and base duty per liter. They do not legally classify the wine; confirm the actual product with the importer and customs broker.

Per-shipment file

  • Commercial invoice with accurate value, currency, terms, origin and SKU detail
  • Packing list, lot/case/pallet counts, gross/net weights
  • Bill of lading or air waybill, booking and arrival data
  • Italian export declaration/MRN and EMCS records where applicable
  • FDA Prior Notice confirmation
  • Customs entry data, HTS/Chapter 99, bond, duties/taxes and delivery order
Prior Notice timing: FDA’s minimum filing windows vary by mode—generally at least 8 hours before arrival by water, 4 hours by air or rail, and 2 hours by road. System-specific maximum filing windows also apply. The broker/forwarder should coordinate the actual arrival data; last-minute changes can require correction.
Commercial samples
Samples are regulated alcohol imports—not ordinary parcels.

A TTB-permitted importer may request a COLA waiver for qualifying samples used solely for trade shows or soliciting orders. Conditions include proper entry, taxes/duties, required health/sulfite information, and “not for sale” labeling. State/event rules can still apply.

First-shipment discipline
Do a document audit before the wine leaves Italy.

Match label, COLA, invoice description, producer/bottler identity, ABV, bottle size, product code/HTS, lots, cases, Prior Notice, origin, and state destination. A small mismatch can trigger holds, relabeling, storage, or refusal.

What an importer-ready winery package should contain

01 · ONE-PAGE FIT

Why this portfolio?

Winery story, appellation, differentiator, target customer, target states, price position, and why the specific importer is a match.

Vintage, designation, varietals, ABV, bottle/case, UPC, ex-cellar price, MOQ, availability, target retail, and launch allocation.

03 · TECHNICAL FILE

Facilities, FDA status, formula data, analyses, claims/certifications, label PDFs, traceability, and export-document capability.

Market evidence

Current sales, export markets, reorders, accounts, critic/press evidence, awards, photography, trade references, and testimonials.

05 · LAUNCH PLAN

90-day activation

Samples, owner/winemaker availability, training, events, target accounts, PR, digital support, and measurable commitments.

06 · ECONOMICS

Margin waterfall

Transparent landed-cost assumptions and viable importer/distributor/retailer pricing under current tariff and freight scenarios.

Can it ship?

Production timeline, mixed-pallet terms, pallet specifications, forwarder, documents, labels, insurance, and first-shipment window.

08 · DECISION ASK

Clear next step

Offer sample tasting, suggested initial order, target launch states, desired relationship, and a realistic decision timetable.

Outreach principle: do not send a generic catalog to every importer. Match the winery to a small set of portfolios and explain the commercial gap it fills. The advisor’s value is qualification, positioning, warm introductions, follow-through, and protecting both sides from an unworkable landed cost or legal structure.
11 · Red flags

Issues to resolve before making introductions

Existing U.S. rights are unclearPrior importer, distributor, broker, family company, or agent may claim territory, state registrations, COLAs, or exclusivity.
Brand ownership is not documentedThe winery cannot prove it controls the marks, labels, photography, or authority to appoint a U.S. partner.
Price cannot survive the channelTarget retail ignores freight, tariffs, tax, compliance, importer/distributor margin, promotions, and state costs.
Technical data is incompleteNo reliable ABV, CO₂, SO₂, formulation, producer/bottler, lot, appellation, or certification records.
Unsupported claims“Organic,” “natural,” “no sulfites,” “healthy,” “low sugar,” estate, vintage, varietal, or geographic claims lack required evidence.
No U.S.-label flexibilityThe winery refuses a compliant back label, importer statement, warning, origin marking, or removal of problematic claims.
No launch resourcesLittle sample inventory, no travel/training budget, no content, no point-of-sale support, and unrealistic expectation that the importer creates demand alone.
Volume promises are not realInventory, vintage continuity, MOQ, lead time, or allocation cannot support the forecast—or the winery expects immediate national scale.
Direct-shipping assumptionsThe winery expects to mail commercial wine nationwide to consumers, retailers, or restaurants without licensed importer/state pathways.
